Chapter 4: The water cycle

Revision notes · Unit 2 · about 12 minutes to read

Water moves between the oceans, the air and the land in a continuous loop. The sun supplies the energy; gravity brings the water back down. These notes cover the four main stages and the words examiners expect to see.

1. Evaporation

Heat from the sun turns liquid water at the surface of oceans, lakes and rivers into water vapour. Warm, dry and windy days speed it up. Plants add more vapour through transpiration from their leaves.

2. Condensation

As moist air rises it cools. Cooler air holds less vapour, so the vapour condenses on tiny dust particles and forms the droplets we see as clouds and fog.

Exam tip: name the process and give the cause, for example "condensation happens because rising air cools".

3. Precipitation

Droplets join together until they are too heavy to stay in the cloud and fall as rain, sleet, snow or hail.

4. Collection

  • Surface run-off flows into streams and rivers.
  • Infiltration soaks water into the soil.
  • Groundwater flow moves it slowly back to the sea.
