Home Composting for Beginners

By Mira Thomas · Garden basics

Home composting turns kitchen scraps and garden waste into rich, dark compost. A compost heap needs only four things: greens, browns, air and water. Get the balance right and the heap does the work for you.

Greens and browns

Greens are fresh, wet materials such as vegetable peelings, coffee grounds and grass clippings. Browns are dry materials such as dead leaves, straw and torn cardboard. Aim for roughly two buckets of browns for every bucket of greens.

Too many greens make a wet, smelly heap. Too many browns make a dry heap that barely changes. Mixing greens and browns in thin layers keeps the compost heap working.

Turning the compost heap

Turn the compost heap every two weeks with a garden fork. Turning adds air, and the microbes that break down kitchen scraps need air to work quickly.

  • Keep the heap as damp as a wrung-out sponge.
  • Chop large scraps into small pieces.
  • Never add meat, dairy or cooked food.

When is compost ready?

Finished compost is dark, crumbly and smells like a forest floor. Most home composting takes six to twelve months. Spread the compost on beds or mix it into potting soil.
