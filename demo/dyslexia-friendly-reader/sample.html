A beginner's guide to baking rye bread

Demo article for testing. Bakery Journal, 20 October. Rye is a little different from wheat, but it is not hard to bake at home.

Why rye feels different

Rye flour has very little gluten. Gluten is what makes wheat dough stretchy, so rye dough feels more like thick clay than bread dough. That is normal. You don't need to knead it for long, and you shouldn't expect it to rise as high as a white loaf.

Instead of gluten, rye holds its shape with natural gums called pentosans. They soak up a lot of water, which is why rye bread stays moist for days.

What you need

  • 500 g dark rye flour
  • 400 ml warm water
  • 100 g active rye starter
  • 10 g salt

Step by step

  1. Mix the starter, water and half of the flour. Cover and leave it for twelve hours.
  2. Add the rest of the flour and the salt. Stir with a wet spoon until no dry flour is left.
  3. Press the dough into a greased tin and smooth the top with wet hands.
  4. Let it rise for two hours, until small cracks appear on top.
  5. Bake at 230 °C for 15 minutes, then at 200 °C for another 45 minutes.
Wait a full day before you slice it. Rye keeps setting as it cools, and a warm loaf will feel gummy inside.
