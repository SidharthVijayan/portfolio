Monsoon brings record rain to the valley

The valley saw its wettest July day in forty years, and farmers say the timing could not have been better for the paddy crop.

By Meera Thomas · 22 July · 4 min read

SharePostEmail

Rain gauges at the district weather station recorded 212 millimetres in the twenty-four hours to eight o'clock on Monday morning, the highest single-day total since the station's records were moved to the hill site in 1985.

The river, which usually runs well below the old stone bridge at this time of year, rose by two metres overnight. Officials closed the lower road for six hours as a precaution, and reopened it by the afternoon once the water had dropped.

Good news for the paddy crop

Farmers in the lower valley had been waiting for steady rain to transplant their seedlings. "Two weeks ago we were worried," said Ravi Menon, who farms four acres near the river. "Now the fields are full and we can start planting on Thursday."

The agriculture office said the reservoirs upstream are now at sixty-eight percent of capacity, compared with forty-one percent at the same time last year, which should keep the canals running well into the dry season.

What to expect this week

Forecasters expect lighter showers for the rest of the week, with another band of heavy rain possible on Sunday. Residents near the river banks are advised to keep an eye on the district alerts and to avoid the lower road after dark.

  • Lower road: open, but may close again at short notice
  • Schools: open as normal
  • Ferry: suspended until Wednesday
