Why we rest our dough overnight

Demo article for testing. Bakery Journal, 12 October

The decision to implement an extended overnight fermentation methodology across our entire production schedule was fundamentally motivated by a comprehensive evaluation of flavour development, crumb structure and digestibility considerations that our bakers had been informally discussing for a considerable period before the transition was formally approved.

Prolonged fermentation at refrigerated temperatures facilitates enzymatic activity that progressively converts complex carbohydrates into simpler sugars, which subsequently contribute to caramelisation during baking and consequently produce a noticeably darker, more aromatic crust. Additionally, the organic acids generated by lactic acid bacteria during this interval considerably enhance the complexity of the flavour profile.

Customers occasionally enquire whether this methodology necessitates additional preservatives, and we would emphasise that the opposite is in fact the case, since the naturally increased acidity of slowly fermented dough inherently inhibits mould development and therefore extends the practical shelf life of every loaf without any supplementary ingredients whatsoever.

  • Considerably improved digestibility for individuals with particular sensitivities.
  • Substantially enhanced aromatic complexity and crust coloration.
  • Naturally extended freshness without artificial preservatives.

Operationally, the overnight schedule necessitated reorganisation of staffing responsibilities, refrigeration capacity and delivery logistics. The transition required approximately three months. Our team considers the investment entirely worthwhile.
